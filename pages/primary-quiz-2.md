## 2.
::: problem id=2_1 marks=1
<!--- JMC 2006 (3) --->
Among the children in a certain family, each child has at least one brother and at least one sister.  

What is the _smallest_ possible number of children in the family?  

* [ ] 2
* [ ] 3
* [x] 4
* [ ] 5
* [ ] 6
{.col-5}

---

There must be at least two boys and two girls in the family.  

If, for example, there was exactly one boy, then that child would not have a brother.  

The same argument applies to girls.
:::


## 3.
::: problem id=3_1 marks=1
<!--- JMC 2004 (5) --->
The word 'thirty' contains 6 letters and 6 = 30 ÷ 5.  

Similarly, the word 'forty' contains five letters and 5 = 40 ÷ 8.  

Which of the following is not a multiple of the numbers of letters it contains?  

* [ ] Six
* [ ] Twelve
* [x] Eighteen
* [ ] Seventy
* [ ] Ninety
{.col-5}

--- 

Eighteen has 8 letters and 18 is not a multiple of 8.  

Of the other options, 6 is a multiple of 3, 12 is a multiple of 6, 70 is a multiple of 7 and 90 is a multiple of 6.
:::


## 4.
<!--- 2018 (8) --->
::: problem id=2_1 marks=1
![](/resources/primary-quiz-2/4-squares.png){image align="right"} 
A figure is made up of three squares.  

The side length of the smallest square is 6 cm.  

How long is the side length of the biggest square?

* [ ] 8 cm
* [ ] 10 cm
* [x] 12 cm
* [ ] 14 cm
* [ ] 16 cm
{.col-5}

---

The side length of the medium square is 2 cm more than the side length of the smallest square. Therefore, the medium square, in the top left, has side length 2 + 6 = 8 cm.  

Next, the part of the smallest square that is not hanging over the edge of the largest square is 2 cm less than the side length of the smallest square, that is 6 – 2 = 4 cm.  

Then, the side length of the largest square is equal to 4 cm plus the side length of the medium square, that is 8 + 4 = 12 cm.  
:::



## 5.
<!--- 2018 (19) --->
::: problem id=5_1 marks=2
Four ladybirds each sit on a different cell of a 4 x 4 grid. One is asleep and does not move.  

On a whistle the other three each move to an adjacent free cell.  

They can crawl up, down, to the right or to the left but are not allowed on any account to move back to the cell that they have just come from.  

![](/resources/primary-quiz-2/5-ladybird.png){image align="center"} 

Where could the ladybirds be after the fourth whistle?   

![](/resources/primary-quiz-2/5-ladybird-options.png){image align="center"} 

* [x] A
* [ ] B
* [ ] C
* [ ] D
* [ ] E
{.col-5}

---

The ladybird in the row second from the bottom and the column second from the left doesn’t change its position in the first four grids. This is the ladybird that is asleep.  

Therefore, E is impossible since the asleep ladybird has moved from the grid after the third whistle.  

D is impossible since the ladybird in the bottom row of the grid after the first whistle has moved two cells.  

C is impossible since ladybirds the highest ladybird has moved along a diagonal. The ladybirds can only move up, down, left or right.  

B is impossible since the ladybird furthest to the right has moved back to a cell that it visited immediately before this move.  
:::
